Quick word about this group: because we're small, this won't feel like a class. It's closer to coaching. We'll spend most of our time on your real work situations, and my goal is simple: the expressions you learn each day should actually come out of your mouth in a real meeting.

Here's a heads up about this cohort's concept. We're not doing textbook business English. The theme is **'the English outside the meeting room'**: reading the room, testing reactions, handling a crisis, talking strategy. # Slide 3 · 워밍업 진행 방식

"Okay, warm-up. Here's how it works: I show you the Korean meaning only, the English is hidden. You say the English **out loud**, not in your head. If you're stuck, just say 'pass', we mark it and move on, no pressure. Then I reveal the expression and you say it one more time in your own sentence.

The point isn't to get them all right. It's to feel exactly where you're shaky, so we know which three to lock in today."

# Slide 4 · 워밍업 (한국어 → 영어, 15문항)

각 문항: 한국어 뜻 읽고 3초 → 멤버가 영어로 → 막히면 패스(🔖) → 공개 → "Now say it in your own sentence."

1. (본격 제안 전에 슬쩍 간을 보다) → **Put out feelers**
   > "*Let's put out some feelers with the sales team before we pitch this officially.* Close cousins: *test the waters*, *float the idea quietly*."
2. (행간을 읽다) → **Read between the lines**
   > "*The email says 'no rush,' but read between the lines, they want it this week.* Also: *catch the subtext*."
3. (상황·사람의 의중을 정확히 파악하다) → **Get a clear read on**
   > "*I couldn't get a clear read on the client, were they in or out?* Notice 'read' as a noun. Same family: *size up*."
4. (반응 보려고 슬쩍 띄워보는 시안) → **Trial balloon**
   > "*Let's float a trial balloon in the all-hands.* Verb is always 'float'. Politics and business both love this one."
5. (공식 라인 밖 비공식 채널로 조율하다) → **Backchannel**
   > "*The deal was stuck in legal, so we backchanneled with their VP.* Diplomatic word turned office essential."
6. (확답 아닌 조건부 약속) → **Soft commit**
   > "*I can give you a soft commit for Friday, I'll confirm after the review.* Opposite of a hard commit."
7. (액면 그대로) → **At face value**
   > "*Don't take the survey results at face value, the sample was tiny.* From the face value of a banknote."
8. (논점 흐리는 미끼, 헛다리) → **Red herring**
   > "*The server error was a red herring, the real bug was in payments.* Debugging conversations live on this word."
9. (위기를 가장 먼저 알리는 조기 신호) → **Canary in the coal mine**
   > "*Churn among our oldest customers is the canary in the coal mine.* Tech even has 'canary releases'."
10. (단서를 달자면) → **With a caveat**
    > "*I'm on board, with one caveat: we revisit the budget in Q3.* The professional way to say 'yes, but'."
11. (결정 대기로 빙빙 도는 중) → **Holding pattern**
    > "*The project's in a holding pattern until legal signs off.* Like a plane circling the airport."
12. (빠져나갈·조정할 여지를 남기다) → **Leave wiggle room**
    > "*Quote them three weeks, but leave some wiggle room for QA.* Room to wiggle: flexibility."
13. (이미 진행 중인) → **In flight**
    > "*We have three initiatives in flight, anything new waits.* Already took off, hard to stop mid-air."
14. (그 일을 처리할 여력이 있다) → **Have the cycles**
    > "*I'd love to help, but I don't have the cycles this sprint.* From CPU cycles, very tech-company."
15. (급한 순서대로 분류·처치하다) → **Triaging**
    > "*We spent the morning triaging bug reports.* Straight from the emergency room."

> "Out of fifteen, how many came out instantly? The passes are exactly what we lock in now."

# Slide 5 · 미니 강의 · 본론 전에 쓰는 표현 6개 (칠판)

"Now let's slow down on six expressions you'll use the most. For each: when to reach for it, and the small nuance that makes it sound native.

**1. Put out feelers**
> The key idea: nothing is official yet. You're extending antennae. Use it before a proposal, a job change, a price change. *'Before we commit, let me put out some feelers.'* The beauty: if the answer is no, nothing is on the record.

**2. Trial balloon**
> Same family, different tool. A feeler is private, one-on-one. A trial balloon is semi-public: you float a version of the idea to a group and watch the reaction. *'That pricing slide was just a trial balloon.'*

**3. Backchannel**
> The unofficial line. When the official process is stuck, you talk to a person, not a process. Careful: it's powerful but political. *'We backchanneled with their VP to unstick the deal.'*

**4. Red herring**
> Your defense word. When a discussion chases the wrong lead: *'I think the server issue is a red herring. The real problem is onboarding.'* Instantly refocuses the room without blaming anyone.

**5. Holding pattern**
> The elegant way to say 'we're stuck waiting.' Not failed, not cancelled, just circling. *'Hiring is in a holding pattern until the budget lands.'* Much better than 'nothing is happening.'

**6. Triaging**
> The word that makes you sound like you own your workload. Not 'I'm busy' but *'I'm triaging, this one's first, that one waits.'* It signals judgment, not just effort.

Now your turn: each of you, pick three of the six and make one sentence about your own real work. Look at the board if you get stuck."
