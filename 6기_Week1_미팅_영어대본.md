## 슬라이드 4 · 워밍업 (한국어 → 영어 즉답, 12문항)

각 문항: 한국어만 읽고 3초 → 학생이 영어로 → 막히면 패스(🔖) → 정답 공개 → "Now say it in your own sentence."

1. (사람을 끌어들여 합류시키다) → **Pull in**
   > "Right, 'pull in'. As in: *Let me pull in the regional managers first.* You can also say *bring in* or *loop in*."
2. (전체 상황·판세를 파악하다) → **Get the lay of the land**
   > "*Get the lay of the land.* *Give me a week to get the lay of the land before I suggest changes.* Close cousins: *get a feel for things*, *size up the situation*."
3. (기대치를 같은 선에 맞추다) → **Level-set**
   > "*Level-set.* *Before we dive in, let's level-set on what success looks like.* Same idea: *get everyone on the same page*, *align expectations*."
4. (대략적인 추정치) → **Ballpark**
   > "*Ballpark.* *Can you give me a ballpark for how long this will take?* Synonyms: *rough estimate*, *a ballpark figure*."
5. (뼈대·틀만 임시로 만들다) → **Stub out**
   > "*Stub out.* *Let me stub out the deck with placeholder slides, then we fill in the content.* Also: *mock up*, *put a placeholder in*."
6. (한 건의 총괄 담당을 맡다) → **Run point**
   > "*Run point.* *Who's running point on the launch?* Same as: *take the lead*, *own it*."
7. (자유롭게 아이디어를 쏟아내다) → **Brainstorm**
   > "*Brainstorm.* *Let's brainstorm a few options before we commit.* Also: *throw ideas around*, *ideate*."
8. (화이트보드에 펼쳐 같이 구상하다) → **Whiteboard**
   > "*Whiteboard*, as a verb. *Can we whiteboard this flow together tomorrow?* Same: *sketch it out*, *map it out*."
9. (기본으로 깔고 가야 하는 최소 전제) → **Table stakes**
   > "*Table stakes.* *Fast shipping isn't a perk anymore, it's table stakes.* Synonyms: *the bare minimum*, *the cost of entry*."
10. (시간·자원을 따로 떼어 확보하다) → **Carve out**
    > "*Carve out.* *Let's carve out thirty minutes on Friday for this.* Also: *set aside*, *block off*."
11. (준비를 다 끝내 정렬해두다) → **Get your ducks in a row**
    > "*Get your ducks in a row.* *Let's get our ducks in a row before the client call.* Same: *get everything lined up*, *be fully prepped*."
12. (일단 멈추고 나중에 다시 다루다) → **Put a pin in it**
    > "*Put a pin in it.* *Good point, but let's put a pin in it and come back after lunch.* Also: *park it for now*, *revisit later*."

> "Out of twelve, how many came out instantly? The ones you passed are exactly what we lock in today."

## 슬라이드 5 · 유의어 강의 (★ 내가 강의하는 핵심 파트, 자세히)

> "Now let's slow down on six expressions that you two will actually use at work. I'll do three for HR, three for hospitality. For each one I'll give you the meaning, when to reach for it, the synonym, and the small nuance that makes it sound natural."

### 🧑‍💼 주혜님 · HR / 온보딩

**1. Level-set**
> "'Level-set' means getting everyone to the same baseline of understanding before you start. In HR you use it constantly: new manager, new policy, new region. You're making sure nobody is starting from a different assumption.
> Example: *Before we roll out the new onboarding, let's level-set on what 'good onboarding' even means.*
> Synonym: *get everyone on the same page*. The nuance: 'level-set' sounds more deliberate and professional, like you're running the meeting. 'On the same page' is softer and more casual. In a kickoff, 'level-set' lands stronger."

**2. Pull in**
> "'Pull in' is bringing the right person into a conversation at the right time. HR is all about this: legal, regional managers, a hiring manager.
> Example: *Let me pull in the regional managers before we finalize the policy.*
> Synonyms: *bring in*, *loop in*. Nuance: 'pull in' feels active, like you're driving it. 'Loop in' is gentler, often just keeping someone informed by email. So 'pull in' for 'I need you in this meeting', 'loop in' for 'just keeping you posted'."

**3. Table stakes**
> "'Table stakes' is the bare minimum just to be in the game, not a bonus, the baseline everyone expects. Great for HR when you talk about culture or communication.
> Example: *For a distributed team, clear communication isn't a perk, it's table stakes.*
> Synonym: *the bare minimum*. Nuance: 'table stakes' sounds strategic and senior, it frames something as non-negotiable. Use it when you want to say 'this is simply expected, full stop.'"

### 🛳️ 도현님 · 승무원 / 호스피탈리티

**4. Run point**
> "'Run point' means being the single person in charge of one thing. On a ship, roles need to be crystal clear, so this is perfect.
> Example: *I'll run point on the excursion desk this week.*
> Synonym: *take the lead*. Nuance: 'run point' is sharper, it signals one clear owner, very useful in operations where confusion costs money. 'Take the lead' is a bit more general."

**5. Get your ducks in a row**
> "This means getting everything prepared and lined up before something happens. Hospitality lives or dies on prep, so this fits your world exactly.
> Example: *Let's get our ducks in a row before the guests board.*
> Synonym: *get everything prepped / lined up*. Nuance: it's friendly and idiomatic, it makes you sound like a native speaker. Use it casually with crew, maybe not in a very formal written report."

**6. Carve out**
> "'Carve out' is deliberately setting aside time or resources, protecting it so it doesn't get eaten by other tasks.
> Example: *I'll carve out time each morning to drill these phrases.*
> Synonyms: *set aside*, *block off*. Nuance: 'carve out' implies effort, like you're cutting this time out of a busy schedule on purpose. Stronger and more intentional than just 'find some time.'"

> "Now your turn. Each of you, take your three and make one sentence about your own real work. If you get stuck, look at the board."
